CIVICSYNC
Public works, in sync
Hackathon project overview


1. THE PROJECT IN ONE MINUTE

CivicSync is a Kerala-focused civic information and community service web
application. A resident can verify a six-digit Kerala postal PIN, see public
works and notices published for that area, submit and track a community
improvement petition, or request household waste-bag pickup. Authorized staff
use a secure Control Room to manage public project information, petitions, and
pickup assignments.

The idea is to put useful, locality-specific civic information in one clear
place and make it easier for residents and public-service teams to understand
what is happening and what needs attention.

Important: CivicSync is a hackathon prototype, not an official government
portal. It does not currently connect to government project-management systems.
Public works appear only after authorized users enter and publish them in
CivicSync.


2. THE PROBLEM

- Civic information can be scattered across department websites, notices, and
  social channels.
- Residents may not know which works are planned or underway near their home.
- It can be difficult to communicate a local concern and understand what
  happens after it is submitted.
- Project information is more useful when residents can find it by locality
  instead of searching a broad statewide list.


3. THE PROPOSED SOLUTION

CivicSync connects an area lookup, local public-work listings, public project
updates, and a role-based petition workflow in one responsive web interface.
The location flow is designed around Kerala PIN codes and Indian postal-area
names.

Primary audiences:
- Residents who want to see local public works and submit or track a concern.
- Residents who submit petitions after adding a private name and date of birth.
- ASHA Worker accounts assigned household waste-pickup requests.
- Department officials and coordinators who manage project information within
  their permissions.
- Administrators who manage staff access, departments, categories, and petition
  decisions.


4. WHAT THE PROTOTYPE DOES

Resident experience:
- Checks a six-digit PIN through a Supabase Edge Function and a third-party
  postal lookup service; only PINs verified as belonging to Kerala are accepted.
- Shows published public works for the verified PIN, with locality, department,
  category, status, dates, and expected disruption details.
- Provides search and filters for project text, locality, status, and department.
- Shows project timelines and public notices when staff have published them.
- Flags overlapping schedules for the same locality as a planning note. This is
  not an official determination that projects conflict.
- Supports email sign-in and secondary phone SMS OTP verification through
  Supabase Auth.
- Stores resident name and date of birth in a private profile table; date of
  birth is self-reported and is not matched to a government identity source.
- Provides account-private petition viewing and progress status for the
  submitting account and authorized reviewers.
- Petition status changes are synchronized to the resident view. Admin approval
  atomically creates a linked, unpublished draft public-work project.
- Residents can request household waste-bag pickups with locality, address,
  bag count, and instructions. Pickup addresses are private to the resident,
  administrators, and the assigned worker.
- Assigned ASHA Workers have a dedicated route panel with ordered statuses:
  assigned, on the way, at the home, bag picked up, and completed. Status changes
  are synchronized to the resident's pickup tracker.
- Offers light and dark themes.

Staff experience:
- The Control Room supports public-work creation and editing, draft/publication
  state, petition review, checkpoint authoring, checkpoint approval, and
  household pickup assignments.
- Department officials can create project records; coordinator/admin workflows
  can publish projects according to the application rules.
- Admins can add, rename, and remove departments, subject to record usage.
- Admins can add and edit project/petition categories, choose where a category
  is used, hide or restore it for new forms, and delete it when it is unused.
- Locality suggestions combine verified postal-office names with localities
  already entered for that PIN.
- Admins can assign roles to existing CivicSync accounts by email when the
  relevant database migration is installed, including the ASHA Worker role.
- Coordinators and Admins can approve private project checkpoints for display
  on the public project timeline.

Petition workflow:
- Resident accounts (including Moderator accounts) can submit petitions after
  completing private name/date-of-birth details and verifying phone access by
  SMS OTP.
- Admin accounts cannot submit petitions.
- Admins can review petitions from other accounts, but cannot review their own.
- Admin approval creates a linked draft CivicSync public-work record; it remains
  unpublished until staff select an appropriate status and publish it.
- A CivicSync draft is not a government commitment, funding decision, or
  official schedule.
- Petition details are intended to be visible only to the submitting account
  and authorized reviewers, enforced through Supabase Row Level Security (RLS).

Household waste-pickup workflow:
- Verified resident accounts submit a private pickup request for their Kerala
  PIN and household address.
- Admins assign the request to an existing ASHA Worker account.
- The assigned worker updates the request in ordered steps. The resident sees
  those updates in their pickup tracker.
- The ASHA Worker label is an application role assigned by CivicSync
  administrators; the prototype does not verify employment or represent an
  official ASHA program.


5. HOW IT WORKS

1. The resident enters a Kerala PIN.
2. The front end invokes the verify-kerala-pin Supabase Edge Function.
3. The function checks a short-lived database cache or queries the postal lookup
   service, filters for Kerala, and caches verified PIN details.
4. CivicSync loads published project records and public notices associated with
   that PIN from Supabase.
5. A resident signs in with email OTP and adds a private name and date of birth
   before submitting a petition. Household pickup additionally requires phone
   SMS OTP verification.
6. An Admin reviews a petition from another account. Approval creates a linked
   unpublished draft project; staff can add private checkpoints and approve
   them for the public timeline.
7. For household pickup, an Admin assigns an ASHA Worker; the worker advances
   the pickup status and the resident tracker receives synchronized updates.

Core data includes user profiles and roles, departments, verified PIN records,
public works, project timeline events, petitions, notifications, audit logs,
and project categories.


6. TECHNOLOGY

- Front end: React with JSX, JavaScript, CSS, and Vite.
- Backend services: Supabase Auth, PostgreSQL database, Row Level Security,
  RPC functions, and Edge Functions.
- Database client: @supabase/supabase-js.
- Location verification: api.postalpincode.in, accessed by a Supabase Edge
  Function rather than directly by the browser.
- Development checks: ESLint and Vite production build.

The browser uses the Supabase URL and publishable key supplied through Vite
environment variables. Do not put a Supabase service-role key in front-end
environment variables or commit private credentials.


7. STRENGTHS / PROS

- Local-first experience: residents search by a familiar Kerala PIN rather than
  navigating a broad catalogue.
- A complete civic workflow concept: area lookup, project visibility, updates,
  petition submission, and staff review are represented in one product.
- Clear responsibility boundaries: resident submissions, staff assignment,
  project/checkpoint publication, and petition review are role-gated.
- Security-aware architecture: sensitive petition access is designed around
  database RLS, and the service-role key is used only server-side by the Edge
  Function.
- Useful staff tools: admins can maintain departments, categories, account
  roles, and locality data without changing application code for every label.
- Better operational awareness: schedule overlaps are surfaced to staff and
  residents as a coordination prompt.
- Responsive UI with light/dark themes and Indian date formatting.
- Private resident verification data and address-scoped pickup access policies.
- Live synchronization for petition decisions, published project checkpoints,
  and waste pickup status changes.
- Prototype status and the absence of a government data connection are
  disclosed in the interface instead of being presented as official data.


8. LIMITATIONS / CONS

- No official government integration: public works are not synced from Kerala
  government or department systems. The displayed information must be entered
  and maintained by authorized CivicSync users.
- Data completeness and accuracy depend on staff. Stale or incorrect records
  could mislead residents unless ownership, review, and update processes are
  established.
- The external postal lookup service is a dependency. Availability, response
  quality, terms, and rate limits are outside CivicSync's control.
- This is a prototype and has not been demonstrated here as a fully audited,
  production-hardened public service.
- Applying the Supabase database migrations is required. Features can fail or
  show missing-table/function errors if database setup is incomplete.
- Role assignment by email depends on the latest workflow migration being
  applied and the Supabase API schema cache being refreshed. If role changes
  return a missing-function/schema-cache error, apply the migrations listed in
  the setup checklist below, then reload the schema cache.
- Phone OTP requires the Supabase phone provider and an SMS provider to be
  enabled and configured for household pickup requests; the prototype does not
  provide the SMS service.
- Name and date of birth are self-reported. Phone OTP proves access to a phone,
  not a person's legal identity or eligibility for an official service.
- There is no application-level petition submission throttle. The existing
  temporary IP-hash limit applies to PIN lookups only.
- Email and push notifications are not implemented. Status changes sync in-app;
  CivicSync does not send pickup or petition updates by email/SMS.
- Petition approval is not a promise that a department will fund, schedule, or
  complete the requested work. Approved petitions create unpublished drafts,
  not government projects.
- Waste collection operations, worker identity/employment, service areas,
  staffing, and collection schedules are not connected to an official provider.
- There is no government identity verification, e-signature, public voting,
  petition threshold, or grievance escalation system in this prototype.
- Operational needs such as department onboarding, moderation policy, data
  retention, backups, support, accessibility testing, and incident response
  still need formal plans.


9. RISKS AND SAFEGUARDS TO DISCUSS

- Misleading or outdated information:
  Require named department owners, timestamps, review procedures, and clear
  source/status labels before a production launch.
- Sensitive petition content:
  Keep RLS enabled, test policies with multiple real test accounts, minimize
  collected personal data, and define retention and deletion procedures.
- Resident profile and household address data:
  Date of birth, verified phone, and precise pickup address are sensitive.
  Restrict database access, avoid showing DOB in staff lists, and set a clear
  retention/deletion policy before real residents use the feature.
- Privilege misuse:
  Keep role changes restricted to administrators, audit changes, and test
  moderator/admin restrictions against the live database policies.
- External service failure:
  Monitor postal lookup errors, retain sensible cache behavior, and provide a
  clear retry path. Do not silently mark an unverified PIN as valid.
- Government-service impersonation:
  Clearly state that CivicSync is independent and that a petition decision is
  not an official government commitment.


10. HACKATHON DEMO FLOW

Suggested 3-minute demonstration:

1. Introduce the problem: residents have difficulty finding what is happening
   near them and where to raise a concern.
2. Enter a valid Kerala PIN, such as 695001, and show the verified postal areas.
3. Browse the project's locality view and demonstrate search/filtering. Use a
   project record already entered in the connected Supabase project; otherwise
   explain that published data appears once staff add it.
4. Open a project to show department, schedule, disruption details, timeline,
   and any schedule-overlap prompt.
5. Sign in with a resident demo account, add the private resident profile, and
   submit a petition. Phone OTP is not required for this step.
6. Switch to a separate Admin demo account, approve that petition, and show the
   linked draft project plus checkpoint approval controls.
7. Show a separate ASHA Worker demo account and move an assigned pickup through
   its status steps; return to the resident account to show synchronized status.
8. Close with the key limitation: the prototype demonstrates civic workflows,
   but official project data and waste-collection operations are not connected.

Do not demonstrate role restrictions using the same account for both roles.
Before the event, test with separate Moderator, Admin, and ordinary-user
accounts. Never display real people's private petition information or
credentials on the projector.


11. SETUP CHECKLIST

Prerequisites:
- Node.js and npm.
- A Supabase project with Auth configured.
- A local .env.local file containing:
    VITE_SUPABASE_URL=<your Supabase project URL>
    VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY=<your Supabase publishable key>

Install and run:
    npm install
    npm run dev

Verify the code:
    npm run lint
    npm run build

Apply the project's SQL migrations in chronological order in the Supabase SQL
Editor, using the files in supabase/migrations:
    20261009200000_civicsync_core.sql
    20261010011500_admin_role_by_email.sql
    20261010012000_seed_kerala_departments.sql
    20261010013000_manage_departments_and_categories.sql
    20261010014000_allow_staff_petition_submissions.sql
    20261010020000_moderator_petition_workflow.sql
    20261010030000_resident_pickups_and_petition_sync.sql
    20261010031000_allow_petitions_without_phone_otp.sql

The moderator workflow migration supersedes the earlier broad petition policy;
the resident/pickup migration enables resident submissions, ASHA Worker pickup
roles, linked petition drafts, and checkpoint review. The final migration lets
residents submit petitions with a private profile without requiring phone OTP;
phone OTP remains required for household pickups. Apply migrations in order,
including both final migrations.
After creating/updating RPC functions, refresh the Supabase API schema cache if
the dashboard reports that a function is missing:
    NOTIFY pgrst, 'reload schema';

Deploy the verify-kerala-pin Edge Function and configure its server-side
Supabase environment secrets according to the Supabase project settings. Do
not add the service-role key to .env.local or the Vite client.

Enable the Supabase Auth phone provider and configure an SMS provider before
using household pickup verification. Email OTP is still used for account
sign-in.

Finally, verify that at least one Admin, resident, and ASHA Worker demo account
exist, departments/categories are populated, and test data is available for the
PIN used in the demonstration. Assign the ASHA Worker role from the Admin
Control Room and assign at least one pickup to that worker.


12. ROADMAP

Near-term:
- Add automated tests for petition, resident profile, pickup, and role policies
  using separate accounts.
- Add better project event authoring and a clear review/publish history.
- Improve loading, empty, and error states for incomplete database setup.
- Add operational monitoring for Edge Function and postal service failures.

Future:
- Integrate official, authorized project data feeds where agreements and APIs
  exist.
- Add verified department ownership and update reminders.
- Add opt-in email/push notifications with consent and delivery controls.
- Add accessibility and Malayalam localization reviews.
- Define governance, privacy, retention, support, and appeals processes.
- Conduct a security review, load testing, backup/restore test, and independent
  usability testing before any public launch.


13. CLOSING PITCH

CivicSync gives residents a locality-first way to understand public works,
raise community needs, and track household pickups, while giving authorized
staff structured tools to review and update the service. The hackathon
prototype demonstrates the workflow; the next step is validating it with
residents and service partners and connecting it to reliable, authorized
operations and public data.
